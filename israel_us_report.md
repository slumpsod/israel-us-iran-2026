## 2. How the Israeli election works, and what happened on 27 October 2026

**The machine (how it works) —** [S1]
- **Nationwide proportional representation on a party list.** The electoral threshold is **3.25%**; seats are handed out in proportion to votes, all 120 seats are contested at once, and **61 are needed for a majority**. No Israeli party has ever won a majority alone, so every government is a coalition.
- **Fixed cycle, with an escape hatch.** The Basic Law requires the national vote on **a Tuesday in the Jewish month of Cheshvan (early Oct to mid-Nov) four years after the last one**. Early elections can be forced: a majority vote of Knesset members, or failure to pass the annual budget bill by 31 March.
- **The 2026 cycle was the first since 1988 that ran its full four-year term with no snap election** — the 2018–2022 crisis (five snap elections in a row) is over. [S2]
- **Mechanics are deliberately low-tech:** paper ballots, party-specific letter slips (reserved letters, e.g. מ ח ل for Likud, ש س for Shas), sealed envelopes, manual counting, then entry into an **air-gapped** computer system. Israel explicitly **does not use electronic voting "due to fears that elections could be vulnerable to cyber threats"**. [S1]
- **Who may vote:** all citizens 18+; citizens living abroad must travel to Israel to vote; booths are even provided on Israeli ships. Overseen by the **Central Elections Committee**. V-Dem downgraded Israel from "liberal democracy" to "electoral democracy" in 2024 and ranked it 53rd on electoral democracy in 2026. [S1]
- Campaign issues: **national security, rising cost of living, military (Haredi) conscription, crime.** [S2]

3. **Premiership loss - not yet final, but the tilt is against him.** `VERIFIED as of 17 Nov 2026`

   - Recommendation tally: **Gadi Eisenkot 67 / Benjamin Netanyahu 46** out of 120 seats. The Joint List stated it voted for Eisenkot "in order to prevent Netanyahu from taking office again".

## 5. The war with Iran - and how it bends both elections

**How it started (`VERIFIED` from [S10, S8, S6])**
- 2025: Trump sent a **letter to Iran's supreme leader Ali Khamenei setting a 60-day deadline** for a deal; talks ran through Oman (12 Apr), Rome (19 Apr), Muscat, plus expert-level sessions (Witkoff/Araghchi; Michael Anton/Takht-Ravanchi).
- 23 June 2025: when the deadline passed, **Israel** launched its **"Twelve-Day War"** (codename **Operation Rising Lion**) against Iranian nuclear targets; Trump called it "the 12 day war" when he announced a ceasefire agreement between Israel and Iran on 23 June 2025 [S10]. The US joined by striking Iranian nuclear facilities in that war [S10].
- Early 2026: **Netanyahu lobbied Trump for a joint military strike on Iran, specifically targeting its leadership** [S6, S8]. January–February 2026 saw **the largest US military buildup in the region since the 2003 Iraq invasion** (roughly 50,000 troops at regional bases).
- **28 February 2026: the war began.** US + Israel flew **hundreds of airstrikes** across Iran, including strikes that **assassinated Supreme Leader Ali Khamenei** and other officials (also Ali Larijani); hundreds of Iranians were killed or injured, the Iranian Red Crescent reported **24 provinces** affected, and UN-commissioned fact-finders later found "reasonable grounds" to believe **US war crimes** occurred, citing two 28 Feb strikes that killed at least 178 civilians [S6].
- Codenames: US **Operation Epic Fury**, Israel **Operation Roaring Lion**, Iran **Operation True Promise IV**; media call it the **Iran war** or "Third Gulf War"; a viral nickname "**Operation Epstein Fury**" emerged because the war began a month after the Epstein files were released (a Washington Post investigation found pro-Iran accounts promoted it) [S6].
- Iran's response: **blockade of the Strait of Hormuz** (claiming sovereignty over it), missile/drone attacks on Israel, US bases and US-allied Gulf states; the UAE, Saudi Arabia, Kuwait and Bahrain then attacked Iran too; Hezbollah-Israel fighting resumed in March and Israel began occupying southern Lebanon; **Mojtaba Khamenei (the assassinated leader's son) was elected supreme leader on 8 March 2026** [S6, S9].

**How it hit the Israeli election** [S6, S2]
- Netanyahu ran the vote in "increasingly apocalyptic" terms (AI mushroom-cloud image over Israel) and pushed defence officials to **escalate the Iran war**, later denying it [S6].
- Israeli polls showed a population unsure who was even winning: 40% "Iran won" / 15% "Israel won" / 43% "no decisive outcome" [S6].
- The war's security-first politics is what the Netanyahu bloc sells on - and the same campaign that produced the "Arab parties stole the election" narrative [S2, S5].

**How it hit the American midterms** `VERIFIED` [S17, S6, S11, S22]
- Timeline of opinion: CNN polling in Sept 2026 - only **26% approved of Trump's handling of Iran**; BBC-cited poll - **63% said the US made the wrong decision** to start the war; NBC exit poll - the war was **a crucial or major factor for 74% of voters**; Fox News + AP-NORC polls (8–12 Oct 2026) - **~60% opposed US military action against Iran** and preferred Democratic candidates, and the war **overtook immigration and crime** as a voter priority [S17, S6].
- **Direct causal evidence:** on **8 October 2026 Trump announced the US military would not resume strikes on Iran before the 3 November midterm elections**, mentioning "productive discussions" with Iran [S6]. Analysts in [S17] argue domestic pressure from fuel prices was the primary political driver behind the administration pushing a ceasefire ahead of the midterms.
- After the loss, Trump acknowledged the war's role: told 60 Minutes on 8 Nov 2026 that **"great dislike" toward Israel, opposition to the Iran war, and rising gas prices** were main reasons for the Republican defeat; Vance blamed the war and called it "a worthwhile cost" only if you accept "**losing the Senate for the next 6 years**" [S17, S24, S22].
- It also broke the 2024 promise: Trump campaigned on **"no new wars"**; Reuters noted it, and BBC reported many Republicans saw the war as a broken promise [S6, S17].
- Some Republicans and White House officials said if the conflict had ended sooner "the results of the midterm elections could have been different" [S17] `REPORTED`.

## 6. The 2026 American midterms: how they work, and what they do to the G.O.P.

**Mechanics (`VERIFIED` from [S17, S18, S19, S19, S20])**
- **Tuesday 3 November 2026**, in the non-consecutive second term of President Donald Trump: all **435 House seats** (+5 of 6 non-voting delegates) and **35 of 100 Senate seats** were contested, deciding the 120th Congress seated in January 2027.
- **Senate staggering:** seats come up in three classes every two years; **Class 2** (last elected 2020) was up, serving 2027–2033, plus **two special elections** to fill vacancies: Florida (Marco Rubio resigned 20 Jan 2025 to become Secretary of State; interim appointee Ashley Moody) and Ohio (**JD Vance resigned 10 Jan 2025** on becoming Vice President; interim Husted, and Husted **lost to Sherrod Brown**).
- **House primaries** for all 435 seats ran from **3 March to 15 September 2026**; districts were re-apportioned after the 2025–26 redistricting on 2020 census data, with **court-mandated new maps in Ohio and Utah**, mid-cycle redraws in North Carolina and Texas, a post-Prop-50 map in California, and a failed Missouri Republican effort.
- Simultaneously: **39 state/territorial gubernatorial and attorney-general elections**, state legislative, judicial and local races, and **more than 40 ballot measures restricting data-centre development** passed [S20, S17].
- Also on the ballot structurally: the **SAVE America Act** (proof-of-citizenship voter registration) passed the House, then **died in the Senate in June** with four Republicans joining Democrats to block it [S17].

**The scoreboard** `VERIFIED`
- **House:** Democrats took control for the first time since 2020; NBC projects **259 seats** (largest majority since 1990); **40+ Democratic flips**; largest popular-vote margin since the mid-1970s (the article text gives **56% / 42%**, and calls it the biggest House vote-win since **1974** in one place and **1976** in another - a minor internal inconsistency) [S19, S17].

**Why the G.O.P. is different after this** [S17, S24, S25, S26]

## 9. The cyber attacks: three separate things people are blending together

### A. US/Israeli cyber + electronic-attack operations **against Iran** `VERIFIED` [S12, S6]
- Cyber and EW were **built into the opening phase of the war**. US Joint Chiefs chairman **Gen. Dan Caine**: "coordinated **space and cyber operations effectively disrupted communications and sensor networks**" in Iran before the main kinetic strikes - explicitly to leave the adversary "**disrupted, disoriented and confused**".
- On 28 Feb 2026 the strikes were **coupled with cyberattacks on Iranian infrastructure, state media and phone apps**, including defacement of state outlets such as **IRNA**, and Israeli operators compromised the mass-market **BadeSaba prayer app (5m+ downloads)** with Persian push notifications urging Iranians to "lay down your weapons or join the forces of liberation".
- After Iranian broadcast sites were physically damaged, **hackers hijacked at least two TV channels** and aired recorded speeches by Trump and Netanyahu telling Iranians to rise against their regime.
- Electronic warfare **disrupted GPS and ship AIS for more than 1,100 vessels** in the Gulf; Israeli intelligence reportedly held **long-term access to Tehran's traffic cameras and mobile-phone infrastructure**, feeding the targeting chain that killed Khamenei.
- Result: a **near-total Iranian internet blackout, 1–4% of normal connectivity for more than 60 hours** (NetBlocks and others), which also crippled the state's own emergency communications.
- Adjacent kinetic hit on digital infrastructure: **three Amazon Web Services data centres in the UAE were struck**, with S3, EC2 and DynamoDB outages across the Middle East in early March [S6, S11].
- Regional copycats: Mossad-themed Google ad campaigns appeared on Pakistani news sites, and a "**Pakistan Cyber Force**" then hacked Indian ABP News [S12].

### B. Iranian and IRGC-linked cyber attacks **on the United States** `VERIFIED` [S12, S14, S15, S16]
- The first recorded Iranian cyber operation of the war hit **Stryker**, a major US medical-supply company: an **Iran-linked group, "Handala"**, said it seized **50 terabytes of company data** and called it "the beginning of a new chapter in cyber warfare", framing it as retaliation for a strike that killed schoolchildren in Minab (Al Jazeera's own satellite-image investigation found the school **possibly deliberately targeted**; six senior US Democratic senators demanded an investigation) [S16].
- A **joint US-agency advisory (FBI, NSA, Department of Energy, CISA)** warned that hackers affiliated with the Iranian government had targeted **programmable logic controllers (PLCs)** - including devices sold by **Rockwell Automation** - in **energy, water and wastewater utilities and unspecified government facilities**, changing on-screen process data and causing "**operational disruption and financial loss**" [S15, S14].
- Attribution: the group presenting itself as hacktivists is assessed as **"CyberAv3ngers", an advanced persistent threat tied to the IRGC's Cyber Electronic Command"**, noted for **inflating** its claimed attacks; the exploits used were **basic, with no available software patch**, and CISA instead urged mitigations [S14].
- Scale: 15 March 2026 is described as **probably the largest wartime cyberattack on the US in history**. Later, officials assessed Iranian involvement in an attack on **~36 Minnesotan municipal water systems (26–27 July 2026)** - four cities confirmed compromise but **no harm to drinking water** - and by **1 Aug 2026** NYT, citing state and local officials, reported the pattern extended to **at least seven US states** (Michigan second to go public) [S12].
- Also reported: hacking of **FBI director Kash Patel's Gmail account**, and a **Lockheed Martin data breach thought linked to Iranian hacktivists** [S14] `REPORTED`.
- CSIS sorts Iran's activity into four types: **opportunistic disruption; cyber espionage** (Israeli security cameras, battle-damage assessment, spyware disguised as a **bomb-shelter location app** for Israeli citizens); **pre-positioning** for later access; and **cyber-enabled information operations** (hacked digital signage at Israeli railway stations) [S14].
- Not new: Iran did the same in **2013** (a dam outside New York City; **Calpine** in California) and in **November 2023** against US water facilities via an Israeli-made **Unitronics** PLC, defacing screens with "you have been hacked, down with Israel". The bigger unsolved problem is Chinese persistence - **Salt Typhoon** in US telecom networks since 2021–22 and in **National Guard networks** - and an FBI "**major incident**" on an internal surveillance system [S14].
- Iranian cyber reach also targeted navigation: Iran **attacked maritime navigation systems on Didamar Island** (Oman) in Hormuz on 10 Nov 2026, with Reuters reporting three sources familiar but unable to confirm damage [S6].

### C. Cyber/influence operations **inside both elections** `VERIFIED that they happened; the impact claims are DISPUTED`
**Israel** [S1, S2, S5]: the Knesset vote is run on **paper ballots with an air-gapped counting system because Israel fears cyber interference**. After Likud's bloc fell short, Likud alleged GOTV groups used "**illegal mobilization software**" and foreign funding from Turkey/Qatar and demanded a Shin Bet investigation - with **no public proof** and the CEC director rejecting the fraud premise.
**United States** [S17]: X **suspended more than 13,000 accounts linked to Iran, Russia and China on 14 Oct 2026**, days before the vote; Meta reported **Iran-origin Facebook/Instagram accounts spreading AI-generated anti-Republican content** in August 2026; US intelligence officials described Russian covert influence aimed at "injecting chaos" (with **no evidence of voting-equipment tampering**); China's **Spamouflage** network posed as American voters; on 28 Sept 2026 Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth announced a DoD programme against foreign meddling - while Trump's 16 July claims (China hoovered up 220m voter-ID files; "our system falls catastrophically short") were pushed back on by election experts.

## 12. Who benefits from a war with Iran?

The honest answer from the sources: **not the states that started it - they lost votes, seats, treasure and blood. The beneficiaries are commercial sectors and, secondarily, the two leaders' short-term political narratives.** Below, each with the number attached.

**Energy - the biggest and most direct winner** `VERIFIED` [S31, S32]
- About **one-fifth of the world's oil and LNG** normally moves through the Hormuz; tanker traffic fell **~90%** at the outset [S32].
- **Brent briefly touched $126/barrel** (a 4-year high), then settled back toward ~$72 [S31].
- **Saudi Aramco's Q1 2026 profit +25% to $32.5bn** - it used its 1,200 km East-West pipeline to the Red Sea to bypass the strait and hold 7m barrels/day while selling into a price spike.
- **BP Q1 $3.2bn** (vs $2.67bn expected); **Shell $6.9bn** vs $5.6bn despite losing its Pearl GTL plant to a strike on Ras Laffan; **TotalEnergies $5.4bn** vs $4.2bn despite 15% of global production shut in, routing via the Fujairah terminal.
- European majors (BP, Eni, Equinor, Repsol, Shell, TotalEnergies) at **$89bn combined over nine months, +102% vs 2025**; Global Witness's investigator called the profits "obscene" as the EU weighed taxing windfall profits [S11].
- **US LNG and shale firms (Venture Global, Cheniere)** are positioned to gain as buyers seek secure supply [S31]. Rystad's analyst: "Each one of those players is going to end up in a net positive place if we see higher prices persist through the year."
- Qatar's Ras Laffan was struck and Qatar's LNG output halted; **QatarEnergy then pivoted to buying US LNG through 2054** [S11] - a winner-by-necessity.

**Defence manufacturing - the second winner** `VERIFIED` [S31, S34, S33]
- Stock markets for arms-makers rose within days of the strikes: **Northrop +5%, RTX +4.5%, Lockheed +3%** [S34]; **BAE Systems +6%** on the London market [S32]; Boeing/RTX/L3Harris/Northrop gave the strongest full-year guidance revisions [S31].
- **Boeing Q1 revenue +14% to $22.2bn**, net loss narrowing from $31m to $7m; **Northrop's order backlog hit a record $95.6bn** [S31].
- **Northrop, Lockheed, RTX, Boeing, BAE, L3Harris, Honeywell** met at the White House within days of the strikes to ramp production; the US is running **billions of dollars in new orders** [S31, S6].
- Structural context (Quincy Institute + Brown's Costs of War, via [S31]): **$2.4 trillion** in Pentagon contracts to private firms 2020-2024, with **one-third ($771bn) to just five companies**.
- Politics of the budget: Trump approved a **$500bn increase** in defence funding, and Pete Hegseth defended a further **$200bn** request with "**It takes money to kill bad guys**" [S31, S42].

**Freight, insurance, finance** `VERIFIED` [S31]

- Wall Street: volatility meant volume - indices fell at first (Dow −400 points, KOSPI −12%, KSE −9.57%), then defence and gold-linked sectors ran up [S11, S31].

## 17. What should be done to prepare

### A. Institutions and officials (what is already recommended, and what the gaps are)

2. **Election administration.** Keep the low-tech model Israel deliberately uses - paper ballots, manual count, **air-gapped** entry systems, precisely because of cyber threats [S1].
